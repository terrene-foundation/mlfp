Speaker Notes — Lesson 6.7 AI Governance Engineering

Total time: ~75 minutes  ·  11 slides  ·  ~7 min per slide average

Governance as engineering, taught against the installed engine — including its sharpest edge: kailash-pact 0.14.1 auto-approves envelope-less roles and unknown addresses. That fail-open default is stated plainly in this lesson, not papered over: every deny-path demonstration attaches an envelope first. The D/T/R address grammar, the five envelope dimensions, the clearance ladder, and budget cascading all land in code students run.

Slide 1~2 minTitle

AI Governance Engineering

Open with the outcome: "Implement access controls, operating envelopes, budget cascading, and governance tests. 6.5 and 6.6 built agents that act; today we bound what they're allowed to do."

→ "The thesis: governance is code."
Slide 2~6 minGovernance is code

Governance is code, not committee

Walk the four pillars as verbs: access controls that DENY unauthorised access; operating envelopes that RESTRICT agent behaviour; budget cascading that PREVENTS runaway costs; audit trails that PROVE compliance.

Read the green box: "The code IS the governance. If it is not in code, it does not exist. A policy document that no function enforces is a wish, not a control."

→ "The address grammar: D/T/R."
Slide 3~7 minD/T/R addressing

PACT D/T/R addressing

Walk the code. compile_governance() from the ex_7 helpers does two things: loads the org YAML into the engine, then calls apply_governance_specs() so the YAML clearances and envelopes are actually enforced — structure alone compiles but doesn't bind.

Then the address: Address.parse("D1-R1-T1-R1") — Department 1, its head Role, Team 1, its head Role (the Data Analyst). And the single decision call: verify_action(role_address, action) returning a GovernanceVerdict with .allowed, .level, and .reason. Read the printed verdict: "True, auto_approved — read_data is within all constraint dimensions."

→ "What D/T/R means."
Slide 4~6 minD/T/R meaning

PACT D/T/R: Department / Team / Role

Walk the org figure and say the expansion precisely, every time: "D/T/R is Department / Team / Role — a positional address grammar, not three job titles. The address reads like a path: department, role, team, role."

Then the verdict column on the figure: "All four verdicts were measured by calling verify_action on the compiled course org with its YAML envelopes applied. Note the two flavours of allow: auto_approved for routine actions, and the gated verdicts — flagged, held, blocked — as constraints bite."

→ "The envelope itself."
Slide 5~8 minOperating envelopes

Operating envelopes

Walk the envelope figure, then read the bullets. A RoleEnvelope wraps a ConstraintEnvelopeConfig with five dimensions — Financial, Operational, Temporal, Data Access, Communication — attached with engine.set_role_envelope(...). "Attached. Not declared in a YAML and hoped for — the envelope must be set on the role before it means anything."

Then the clearance ladder, said carefully — students always mis-order it: "public < restricted < confidential < secret < top_secret. 'restricted' is the second-lowest rung, not the top; 'internal' is an alias for it. Department heads in the course orgs sit at secret."

The honest default, stated plainly: "The installed engine — kailash-pact 0.14.1 — auto-approves any role with no envelope, and any address not in the org. It is fail-open by default. That's why every deny-path demo in this course attaches an envelope first — and why the common-mistakes slide tells you to reject unknown addresses before calling verify_action."
→ "Money, cascading down the org."
Slide 6~7 minBudget cascading

Budget cascading

Walk the cascade figure: the organisation caps the department, the department caps the team, the team caps the agent.

Then the two layers from the bullets. Organisation layer: FinancialConstraintConfig(max_spend_usd=...) in each envelope — and the monotonic rule: "A child asking for more than its parent fails validate_tightening — 'child max_spend_usd (12.0) exceeds parent (10.0)'. Envelopes only ever tighten down the tree." Agent layer: budget_limit_usd or GovernedSupervisor(budget_usd=...), set at construction — 6.5's rule again. Read the footer: "Dollar figures are illustrative: on local Ollama every call is priced $0."

→ "The governed agent in code."
Slide 7~8 minGovernedSupervisor

GovernedSupervisor

Walk the code. GovernedSupervisor(model=DEFAULT_CHAT_MODEL, budget_usd=50.0, tools=["answer_question", "read_data"], data_clearance="confidential") — the model from OLLAMA_CHAT_MODEL, the budget at construction, the tool whitelist, the clearance ceiling.

Read the teal box: "It's a planner, not a wrapper around your agent: it plans steps and calls your execute_node callback for each, enforcing budget, tools, and clearance at every step. Your callback returns the result plus cost and token counts." Then the audit line: governed.audit.verify_chain() — "Every record is hash-linked to the previous one; verification returns True unless a record was altered. Tamper-evidence by construction."

→ "The verdict taxonomy."
Slide 8~6 minVerdicts

Every action gets a verdict

Walk the verdict figure: every action flows through the engine and emerges with one of four outcomes — auto_approved, flagged, held, blocked. "No silent decisions. Even an approval is a recorded verdict with a reason string — which is what makes the audit trail a compliance artifact and not just a log."

→ "Prove it: governance tests."
Slide 9~8 minGovernance testing

Governance testing

Walk the two tests. First: test_denied_access_stays_denied — the data analyst's envelope allows read / summarise / report only, so verify_action("D1-R1-T1-R1", "deploy_model") must come back blocked and not allowed. Second: the tightening test — a child envelope requesting deploy_model beyond its parent's read_data must raise MonotonicTighteningError from RoleEnvelope.validate_tightening(parent_envelope=..., child_envelope=...) — keyword-only arguments.

Read the amber box — it's the testing principle and the lesson's sharpest edge: "Test what does NOT happen — and only after attaching an envelope. The same deny test against a role with no envelope would fail: the default is auto-approve. A passing deny test proves an envelope is attached, not just that the engine exists."

→ "Common mistakes."
Slide 10~6 minMistakes

Common mistakes

Walk the three. One: assuming the engine is fail-closed — "kailash-pact 0.14.1 auto-approves roles with no envelope and addresses outside the org. Attach an envelope to every agent role, and reject unknown addresses before calling verify_action." Two: testing only happy paths — test denied access, not just allowed access. Three: no audit trail — compliance needs proof; log every access decision.

→ "Recap and bridge to the capstone."
Slide 11~3 minRecap

What you can now do

Walk the checklist: implement D/T/R (Department / Team / Role) addressing and access checks; define and enforce operating envelopes; implement budget cascading; and write governance tests that verify access controls. Tease 6.8: "The capstone — deploy a complete governed AI system via Nexus. The culmination of the entire programme."

→ End of Lesson 6.7. Ten-minute break.
