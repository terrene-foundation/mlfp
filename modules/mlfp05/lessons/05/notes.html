Speaker Notes — Lesson 5.5 Generative Models — GANs and Diffusion

Total time: ~90 minutes  ·  14 slides  ·  ~6 min per slide average

Generation: from understanding data to making it. The lesson's through-line is training stability — the GAN's minimax game is fragile, WGAN-GP is the engineered fix, and diffusion trades sampling speed for robustness. Two things students consistently get wrong that these slides get right: the WGAN critic loss rises toward 0 as quality improves (a rising loss is healthy here), and FID is a distribution-level metric — not a per-image score and not a privacy test.

Slide 1~2 minTitle

Generative Models — GANs and Diffusion

Open with the outcome: "Train a GAN and a WGAN-GP, understand diffusion, evaluate with FID. 5.1's autoencoders compressed data; today we create it."

→ "The game."
Slide 2~5 minGAN setup

The GAN setup — a minimax game

Walk the figure: the generator $G$ turns noise $z$ into fake samples; the discriminator $D$ sees a mix of real and fake and calls out the fakes; $G$ improves by fooling $D$. "Two networks with opposed objectives training each other. The forger and the detective, both getting better — that's the whole architecture."

→ "The objective."
Slide 3~5 minMinimax objective

GAN minimax objective

Walk the formula: $\min_G \max_D$ of the log-probability that $D$ calls real samples real, plus the log-probability it calls fakes fake. "D wants to maximise — correctly classify real and fake. G wants to minimise — fool D into calling fakes real."

Then the training rhythm: "Alternate: update $D$ for $k$ steps, then $G$ for one step. The two players must stay roughly evenly matched — a dominant $D$ starves $G$ of gradient; a dominant $G$ means $D$ has failed."

→ "The generator's architecture."
Slide 4~5 minDCGAN generator

DCGAN generator: progressive upsampling

Walk the figure: the noise vector reshaped to a small spatial map, then transposed convolutions doubling the resolution stage by stage until the full image. "Progressive upsampling — each stage adds detail on top of the coarser layout below. Batch norm throughout, ReLU inside, tanh at the output so pixels land in [−1, 1]."

→ "The failure mode that defines GANs."
Slide 5~5 minMode collapse

Mode collapse — the GAN failure mode

Walk the figure: the real distribution has many modes; the generator's output clusters on one. "The generator finds one output that fools D and repeats it — every noise vector maps to nearly the same image. Losses can look fine while diversity silently dies."

Read the caption: "WGAN-GP reduces this — it does not eliminate it. The defence is monitoring: watch sample diversity, not just the losses."

→ "The better distance."
Slide 6~6 minWGAN

WGAN — Wasserstein distance

Walk the new objective: $D$ (now called the critic — no sigmoid) simply scores real higher than fake; the gap estimates the Wasserstein (Earth Mover's) distance. "The original GAN's JS divergence gets stuck at log 2 when the real and fake supports don't overlap — a flat gradient, no signal. The Wasserstein distance keeps shrinking as the distributions approach, so the critic always has something useful to say."

The Lipschitz constraint the distance requires is enforced by the gradient penalty (WGAN-GP) — next slide's code. Then read the blue box carefully — it's the most-misread fact of the lesson: "Minus the critic loss (without GP) estimates $W$, so the critic loss rises toward 0 as quality improves. A rising critic loss is healthy — it's the distributions converging. You can use it to monitor training, which the original GAN's oscillating losses never allowed."

→ "The other family: diffusion."
Slide 7~5 minDDPM

DDPM — Denoising Diffusion

Walk the two processes in the figure. Forward: gradually add Gaussian noise to data until it's pure noise — a fixed, parameter-free process. Reverse: train a network to remove one step of noise at a time; sampling is iterating that network from pure noise back to an image.

Walk the trade-offs: "More stable than GANs — no adversarial game, just regression against noise. Better diversity — far less prone to mode collapse. The cost: slow sampling, many denoising steps; speed is the active research front."

→ "How do you score generated samples?"
Slide 8~5 minFID

FID — evaluating generated samples

Walk the formula: Fréchet distance between the feature statistics of real and generated sets — means and covariances in a feature space. "Lower FID = closer distributions — it captures fidelity and diversity together. FID = 0 means identical feature statistics."

Two precision points from the bullets. "Usually computed on Inception features; Exercise 5.5 uses a small LeNet, so its FID values compare only with each other — never against published Inception FID. And it's distribution-level, not per-image — and not a privacy test: a model that copies its training images scores beautifully."

→ "The decision table."
Slide 9~4 minWhen to use what

When to use what

Walk the table. VAE (5.1): smooth latent space and interpolation — but blurry outputs. GAN: sharp images and fast sampling — at the price of training instability and mode collapse. Diffusion: best quality and diversity — at the price of slow sampling.

"Quality-versus-speed-versus-stability: pick two. If quality matters more than speed, the modern default is diffusion; if you need fast sampling and can babysit the training, GAN."

→ "The GAN training loop, written out."
Slide 10~6 minGAN loop

GAN training loop (any G / D architecture)

Walk the two halves. Train $D$: maximise $\log D(x) + \log(1 - D(G(z)))$ — code negates it for the minimiser, and note fake.detach(): "when training $D$, the generator is frozen data — no gradient flows into it."

Train $G$: the non-saturating loss — minimise $-\log D(G(z))$. "Why not minimise $\log(1 - D(G(z)))$, the literal minimax? Early in training $D$ wins everywhere, that loss saturates, and $G$ gets no gradient — the same saturation problem as the missing $\sqrt{d_k}$. Maximising $\log D(G(z))$ instead keeps gradients strong when $G$ is weak. The code you see is what every real GAN runs."

→ "The WGAN-GP deltas."
Slide 11~6 minWGAN-GP differences

WGAN-GP training differences

Walk the code. The critic has no sigmoid. The gradient penalty: interpolate real and fake with a random $\alpha$ per sample, score the interpolation, take the gradient of the critic's output with respect to that input, and penalise its deviation from norm 1. Note the comment in the code: "the norm is over the whole input per sample, not per pixel."

Then the critic loss assembly: $E[D(\text{fake})] - E[D(\text{real})] + 10 \cdot \text{gp}$ — the Wasserstein gap plus the penalty. "The penalty replaces weight clipping, the crude first attempt at enforcing Lipschitz. And the training ratio: 5 critic steps per generator step — the critic must stay informative for the generator's gradient to mean anything."

→ "Singapore applications."
Slide 12~4 minSG applications

Singapore applications

Walk the three, with the honesty clauses intact. Synthetic data: generate realistic transactions to enlarge a bank's rare-fraud training set — "synthetic is not private by default; it needs formal guarantees like DP-SGD and memorisation tests before sharing." Data augmentation: extra examples of rare conditions for a hospital imaging classifier — "always evaluate on real images." Simulation: realistic traffic patterns for a transport planner's scenario models.

→ "Common mistakes."
Slide 13~4 minMistakes

Common mistakes

Walk the four. One: training $G$ too aggressively — $D$ must stay informative; WGAN-GP's 5:1 critic ratio exists for this. Two: ignoring mode collapse — monitor sample diversity, not just $D$'s loss. Three: no FID evaluation — visual inspection is subjective; report FID. Four: using GANs when diffusion is better — if quality matters more than speed, use diffusion.

→ "Recap and bridge."
Slide 14~2 minRecap

What you can now do

Walk the checklist: implement GAN and WGAN-GP loops and describe the DCGAN architecture; explain mode collapse and how WGAN addresses it; describe DDPM's forward and reverse processes; evaluate with FID; and select VAE vs GAN vs diffusion for a task. Tease 5.6: "Patterns in connected data — Graph Neural Networks."
