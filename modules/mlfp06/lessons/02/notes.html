Speaker Notes — Lesson 6.2 LoRA, Adapters, and the Fine-Tuning Landscape

Total time: ~75 minutes  ·  20 slides  ·  ~3.5 min per slide average

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning, from first principles to the AlignmentPipeline run. The core insight is one equation — $W = W_0 + BA$ — and the practical heart is the two code slides: LoRA from scratch, then the SFT config and training run on IMDB. Prerequisites matter here: 5.4's transformers and 4.3's SVD are both invoked, so check the room remembers low-rank structure before you start.

Slide 1~1 minTitle

LoRA, Adapters, and the Fine-Tuning Landscape

Open with the outcome: "Run AlignmentPipeline with LoRA by the end of the hour. From scratch first, the 10-technique survey second, model merging and QLoRA to close."

→ "When does prompting stop being enough?"
Slide 2~4 minMotivation

When prompting is not enough

Walk the four symptoms: wrong tone for a regulatory environment; hallucinating Singapore regulations that don't exist; refusing to extract the fields you need; domain vocabulary the pre-trained model has seen too little of. "Prompting steers; it cannot teach. When the gap is knowledge or ingrained style, you fine-tune."

Then the cost problem: "Full fine-tuning updates all ~7B weights — too expensive, and too destructive: it overwrites general capability to teach narrow behaviour." Read the green box: "PEFT: update < 1% of weights. Base knowledge stays intact."

→ "The insight: low rank."
Slide 3~4 minLoRA insight

The LoRA insight

Walk the equation: $W = W_0 + \Delta W = W_0 + BA$, with $r \ll \min(d, k)$. The base $W_0$ — say 4096 × 4096, 16.8 million parameters — is frozen. Learn only $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$.

Do the arithmetic: at $r = 8$, that's $r(d+k) = 65{,}536$ parameters — a 250× reduction. "Fine-tuning updates turn out to have low intrinsic rank — the change a task needs is much smaller than the matrix it lives in. LoRA exploits that. Sound familiar? It's 4.3's low-rank bet, inside a transformer weight."

→ "The picture."
Slide 4~3 minLoRA picture

Picture of LoRA

Walk the diagram: the input flows through two paths — the frozen $W_0$ and the tiny $B \cdot A$ side path — and the outputs sum. "The base model is untouched. The side path is everything you train. At inference you can merge $BA$ back into $W_0$ and the model is literally the same shape as before — zero added latency."

→ "Forward pass and initialisation."
Slide 5~4 minForward + init

Forward pass + init

Walk the forward pass: $\mathbf{y} = W_0\mathbf{x} + \frac{\alpha}{r}BA\mathbf{x}$. The $\alpha/r$ scaling decouples "effect strength" from "rank" — change $r$ without retuning the learning rate. Typical: $r \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$, $\alpha = r$ or $2r$.

Then the initialisation contract: "B is initialised to zero — step 0 is numerically identical to the base model; training starts from the pre-trained behaviour, not from noise. $A$ gets Kaiming uniform to break symmetry. Same trick as 5.7's zero-initialised adapters — the delta starts as nothing."

→ "Write it."
Slide 6~4 minLoRA from scratch

LoRA from scratch — the minimal layer

Walk the code: wrap a base linear layer, freeze its parameters, create lora_A (rank × in_features, Kaiming) and lora_B (out_features × rank, zeros), precompute scaling = alpha / rank. The forward pass is one line: base output plus the $BA$ path, scaled.

"Twenty lines for the technique that fine-tuned half the open models you know. Read the two parameter shapes against the equation until they map — $A$ projects down into the rank space, $B$ projects back up."

→ "The other PEFT: adapter bottlenecks."
Slide 7~3 minAdapters

The other PEFT — adapter bottlenecks

Walk the figure: a small bottleneck module — down-project, non-linearity, up-project — inserted after the attention and FFN of each transformer block, with a residual connection. "Near- identity initialisation: zero the up projection. 5.7's adapter slide, now inside a transformer."

→ "Head to head."
Slide 8~4 minLoRA vs adapters

LoRA vs adapters

Walk the table. Mechanism: $W_0 + BA$ vs a new bottleneck module in the residual stream. Parameters: $r(d+k)$ vs $2dm$. Inference cost: zero for LoRA — it merges into $W_0$ — versus +5–10% latency for adapters. And the one thing adapters win: multiple active adapters in a single forward pass — LoRA's merged weights allow exactly one.

Read the green box: "Default: LoRA. Reach for adapters only when you need multiple active adapters in one forward pass."

→ "The full landscape."
Slide 9~3 minLandscape

The 10-technique landscape

Walk the landscape map: the PEFT family (LoRA, adapters, prefix, prompt), the full-update family (full SFT, layer-wise LR decay, progressive freeze), and the specialist tools (distillation, DP-SGD, EWC). "Ten techniques, three philosophies: adapt a few parameters, update all parameters carefully, or protect something specific — size, privacy, or old skills."

→ "The decision table."
Slide 10~4 minReach table

When to reach for which

Walk the table by constraint. LoRA: default for domain or task adaptation. Adapters: multiple active adapters in one pass. Prefix tuning: task-switching by prefix swap. Prompt tuning: ultra-low parameter regime on huge models. Full SFT: lots of compute and data, when LoRA plateaus. LLRD: full SFT where earlier layers are general — decay the LR by layer. Progressive freeze: small dataset, prevent forgetting. Distillation: compress to a deployable size. DP-SGD: training data has PII. EWC: sequential multi-task without forgetting.

→ "Combining fine-tunes without retraining."
Slide 11~4 minModel merging

Model merging — combining fine-tunes without retraining

Walk the four recipes. TIES: trim the smallest updates, elect the dominant sign, average only sign-aligned updates — kills the interference where two fine-tunes push the same weight opposite ways. DARE: randomly drop 90% of the updates and rescale the survivors by $1/(1-p)$ — sparsification as denoising. SLERP: spherical interpolation between two checkpoints — next slide. Task arithmetic: add or subtract delta vectors as if they were task gradients — "add sentiment, subtract toxicity."

→ "SLERP precisely."
Slide 12~3 minSLERP

SLERP in one formula

Walk the formula: the two checkpoints weighted by sines of the fractions of $\phi$ — the angle between them on the parameter hypersphere. At $t = 0.5$ you get the geodesic midpoint.

"Linear averaging takes the chord through weight space; SLERP follows the arc — smoother, fewer interference artefacts. When a naive 50/50 merge produces a model that's worse at both tasks, SLERP is the first fix to try."

→ "Squeezing memory: quantisation."
Slide 13~4 minQuantisation & QLoRA

Quantisation & QLoRA

Walk the four entries. GPTQ / AWQ: post-training 4-bit, calibrated. GGUF: llama.cpp's format — runs on CPU and Apple Silicon; that's how the course's local models run. bitsandbytes: on-the-fly quantisation during PyTorch training. And QLoRA: 4-bit base plus 16-bit LoRA — fine-tune a 65B model on one 48 GB GPU (Dettmers et al., 2023).

Read the blue box: "Serious LoRA runs on constrained hardware should default to QLoRA. The base sits frozen in 4-bit; only the tiny adapter trains in 16-bit."

→ "The memory layout."
Slide 14~3 minQLoRA layout

QLoRA: 4-bit base + fp16 adapters

Walk the memory diagram: the bulk of the bar is the 4-bit frozen base; the thin sliver on top is the 16-bit trainable adapter. "The 16.8-million-parameter matrix from the LoRA slide lives at 4 bits per weight; the 65-thousand-parameter adapter trains in full precision. Memory is dominated by the base; learning capacity lives in the adapter."

→ "Configure the real pipeline."
Slide 15~4 minAlignmentConfig

Configure AlignmentPipeline

Walk the config. method="sft". The base_model_id comes from the environment — SFT_BASE_MODEL, with the Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct default — "a HuggingFace repo id, read from the environment, never hardcoded; the model is configuration." Then the typed sub-configs: LoRAConfig — rank 16, alpha 32, dropout 0.05, targeting q_proj and v_proj; SFTConfig — 3 epochs, batch 4 with gradient accumulation 4, lr 2e-4, max sequence 512.

"Typed sub-configs: LoRAConfig says what to adapt, SFTConfig says how to train. Exercise 2.6 builds exactly this via build_sft_config()."

→ "Train and register."
Slide 16~4 minTrain + register

Train and register the adapter

Walk the code. Load the IMDB instruction/response pairs from the ex_2 helper; SFT trains on a single text column — instruction, blank line, response — as a HuggingFace Dataset. await AlignmentPipeline(cfg).train(hf_ds, adapter_name="imdb_sentiment_sft_v1") returns the training loss and the adapter path.

Then registration: AdapterRegistry().register_adapter with an AdapterSignature — base model, adapter type, training method — plus the metrics. "The registry is M3.7's ModelRegistry for adapters: every adapter records its base model, method, and metrics. And note the footer: no eval split is passed to train(), so it reports a training loss only — the eval reading is your job, next slide."

→ "Read the curves."
Slide 17~3 minLoss curves

Reading the loss curves

Walk the four diagnoses. Healthy: train falls smoothly, eval follows with a small gap. Overfitting: train falls, eval rises — reduce epochs or raise dropout. Underfitting: both flat — raise the LR or increase the rank. Leakage: eval loss below train loss from step 1 — check the split for overlap.

"Exercise 2.6 logs the training loss; hold out the eval_data from the loader to read both curves. Same U-shape literacy as 3.2 — now applied to a 0.5B model's fine- tune."

→ "The reduction, tabulated."
Slide 18~3 minParameter reduction

Parameter reduction

Walk the formula and the table: $r(d+k)/dk$ on a 4096-square matrix — rank 4 is 0.2% of the base's parameters, rank 8 is 0.4%, 16 is 0.8%, 32 is 1.6%.

Read the footer: "Double the rank, double the parameters. No free lunch — but at these prices, lunch is nearly free. The question is never 'can we afford the rank', it's 'does the task need it' — and the loss curves tell you."

→ "Four mistakes."
Slide 19~3 minMistakes

Four common mistakes

Walk the four red boxes. One: wrong target modules — start with q_proj and v_proj; attention projections are the highest-leverage default. Two: rank too small for the task — plateau early? double $r$. Three: forgetting to load the adapter at inference — the base model alone will happily answer as if training never happened; check the registry call. Four: catastrophic forgetting on small datasets — fewer epochs, lower LR, or progressive freezing.

→ "Recap and bridge."
Slide 20~2 minRecap

What you can now do

Walk the checklist: explain LoRA as $W_0 + BA$ and compute the parameter reduction; implement LoRALayer and AdapterLayer from scratch; choose among the 10 techniques; explain TIES / DARE / SLERP merging; configure AlignmentPipeline and register the adapter; and read the loss curves. Tease 6.3: "The model does what you ask. Does it do what you want? Preference alignment — derive DPO from the RLHF objective and train a safer model."
