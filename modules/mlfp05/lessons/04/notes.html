Speaker Notes — Lesson 5.4 Transformers and Self-Attention

Total time: ~75 minutes  ·  19 slides  ·  ~4 min per slide average

The most important architecture lesson of the course — the Transformer is what M6 is made of. Build it bottom-up: Q/K/V as a database lookup, the scaled dot-product with its load-bearing √d_k, multi-head as parallel viewpoints, positional encoding to cure order-blindness, then assemble with nn.TransformerEncoder. The worked bit task is the proof of concept: a label that depends on where ones appear — without positional encoding the model caps at the 60.8% majority rate.

Slide 1~1 minTitle

Transformers and Self-Attention

Open with the stakes: "5.3 ended with temporal attention — a learned weighted sum over time. Today we remove the recurrence and keep the attention. The result is the architecture behind every model you'll meet in M6: BERT, GPT, T5, ViT."

→ "Why did attention win?"
Slide 2~4 minWhy attention

Why attention replaced recurrence

Walk the three bullets. LSTMs are sequential — to compute $h_5$ you need $h_1$ through $h_4$; no parallelisation across time. Self-attention lets every token look at every other token in parallel. The cost: more total work — every pair of tokens, $O(L^2)$ — but fully parallel across positions.

"GPUs don't care about total work; they care about parallelism. 2017, Vaswani et al., 'Attention is all you need'. NLP has never been the same."

→ "The database metaphor."
Slide 3~4 minQ, K, V

Query, Key, Value — database metaphor

Walk the three roles. Each token emits a Query — what am I looking for? A Key — what do I contain? A Value — what do I return if retrieved?

"Compare queries against keys → similarity scores → softmax → weights → weighted sum of values. It's a fuzzy database lookup, differentiable end to end. The token decides what it's looking for; the sequence answers."

→ "One formula."
Slide 4~4 minScaled dot-product

Scaled dot-product attention

Walk the boxed formula and the four steps. $QK^\top$ produces the L×L similarity matrix — every token against every token. Divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$. Row-wise softmax — each row becomes a probability distribution over keys. Multiply by $V$ — the weighted sum of values.

"Four tensor operations. This one line of math is the entire attention mechanism — everything else in a Transformer is feed- forwards, norms, and plumbing around it."

→ "The one character that makes it train."
Slide 5~4 minWhy √d_k

Why divide by √d_k?

Walk the variance argument: the dot product of two random $d_k$-dimensional vectors has variance $d_k$, so its typical magnitude is $\sqrt{d_k}$ — and it grows with dimension.

"Feed large scores into softmax and it saturates to one-hot — the distribution collapses onto the biggest logit, the gradient through softmax vanishes, training stalls. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ brings the scores back to unit variance. One character in code, load-bearing for training — 5.3's vanishing-gradient lesson, this time inside the attention."

→ "See the weights."
Slide 6~3 minAttention picture

Attention: which words matter for which

Walk the heatmap: rows are queries, columns are keys, brightness is the softmax weight. "Every row is one token's answer to 'what should I look at?' Read a row aloud and it sounds like grammar — subjects attending to verbs, pronouns attending to their referents. The network learned that from the task alone."

→ "Implement it — two einsums."
Slide 7~4 minFrom scratch

From scratch with einsum

Walk the function. einsum("bld,bmd->blm", q, k) is the batched $QK^\top$; divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$; the optional mask fills disallowed positions with −∞ so the softmax zeroes them; softmax over the last dim; the second einsum applies the weights to $V$.

"Two einsum calls, one softmax — the whole operation. If einsum's string notation is new, read it as: batch $b$, query positions $l$, key positions $m$, features $d$. The letters tell you exactly which axes multiply out."

→ "One viewpoint is not enough."
Slide 8~4 minMulti-head

Multi-head attention

Walk the idea: one head is one viewpoint — one similarity geometry. Multi-head runs $h$ attention operations in parallel on smaller subspaces, concatenates, and mixes with $W^O$.

Do the arithmetic: with $d_{\text{model}} = 512$ and $h = 8$ heads, each head operates in $d_k = 64$. "Same total parameters, eight different relationships — one head can track syntax, another coreference, another position. The concat-and-project lets the model recombine what the heads saw."

→ "With the tensor shapes."
Slide 9~3 minMulti-head shapes

Multi-head attention with tensor shapes

Walk the shape flow: (B, L, d_model) splits into (B, h, L, d_k) per head, attention runs per head, then concat back to (B, L, d_model) and project. "Shape discipline is half of Transformer engineering. When your attention breaks, print shapes before you print losses."

→ "The order problem."
Slide 10~4 minPositional encoding

Positional encoding

State the problem first: "Self-attention is permutation- equivariant — order-blind. Shuffle the tokens and the outputs just shuffle the same way. But 'dog bites man' ≠ 'man bites dog'." The fix: add a deterministic position signal to every token embedding.

Walk the two formulas — sine on even dimensions, cosine on odd — and say the frequency direction precisely: "Low dimensions (small $i$) oscillate fastest — they carry fine, local position. High dimensions oscillate slowest — coarse, global position. A stack of frequencies from fast to slow, like the digits of a binary clock. Deterministic — no parameters."

If the formula intimidates: "Each dimension is a hand on a clock, ticking at its own rate. The position is readable from the pattern of hands — fast hands for fine position, slow hands for coarse."
→ "In code."
Slide 11~3 minPE code

PositionalEncoding in PyTorch

Walk the code: precompute the whole table once — position as a column vector, div_term as the $10000^{-2i/d}$ rates; sine into even columns, cosine into odd. register_buffer keeps it with the model without making it a parameter. Forward is one line: add the first x.size(1) rows to the embeddings.

"No training, no gradient — a fixed lookup added to every sequence. The buffer rides along to any device the model moves to."

→ "The repeatable block."
Slide 12~4 minThe block

The block that repeats N times

Walk the block diagram: multi-head attention, add-and-norm, feed-forward, add-and-norm. "Stack $N$ identical blocks. Input and output shapes are identical — (seq_len, d_model) — so they compose like Lego. PyTorch ships it as nn.TransformerEncoderLayer."

Read the parenthetical — it prevents a real confusion: "The diagram shows the original post-norm layout; the code we're about to see uses pre-norm, norm_first=True — norm inside the residual branch, which trains more stably at depth. Same block, different norm placement."

→ "Assemble a tiny one."
Slide 13~4 minTinyTransformer

TinyTransformer in PyTorch

Walk the module: an embedding table, the PositionalEncoding from two slides ago, one nn.TransformerEncoderLayer (d_model 64, 4 heads, FFN 128, batch_first, pre-norm) stacked twice by nn.TransformerEncoder, and a linear head on the mean of the token outputs.

"Embedding → positions → N blocks → pool → classify. A complete, trainable Transformer in about fifteen lines — and every piece is one you built by hand today."

→ "One substitution to notice: layer norm."
Slide 14~3 minLayer norm

Layer norm, not batch norm

Contrast the axes: layer norm normalises across features per token; batch norm normalises across the batch per feature. "Sequence lengths vary → batch statistics become noisy or meaningless — and at inference, a single sentence has no batch. Layer norm is robust to all of that. Always layer norm in Transformers."

→ "The family."
Slide 15~4 minVariants

Transformer variants

Walk the table. BERT: encoder, masked-LM objective — fill in the blank; classification and NER. GPT: decoder, next-token — generation. T5: encoder-decoder, span corruption — translation. ViT: encoder on image patches — 5.2's last slide. Whisper: encoder-decoder, seq2seq next-token — speech.

"One architecture, three wiring patterns. Encoder for understanding, decoder for generation, encoder-decoder for transformation. M6 lives almost entirely in the decoder column — and 5.7's BERT fine-tuning is in the first row."

→ "Prove the pieces matter: the worked task."
Slide 16~4 minWorked bit task

Order-dependent bit task: worked example

Walk the dataset: 3,000 random bit strings of length 20; the label is 1 if the first half holds more ones than the second half. "The label depends on where the ones are — the count alone tells you nothing."

Then the experiment: "Train the TinyTransformer with positional encoding — it solves the task essentially perfectly. Strip the positional encoding and the model becomes a set classifier: it cannot beat ~60.8% — the measured majority-class rate. The positional encoding isn't decoration; on an order- dependent task it's the difference between learning and guessing."

→ "Four traps."
Slide 17~3 minTraps

Four traps

Walk the four. One: forgetting positional encoding — the model becomes a set classifier and caps at the 60.8% majority rate on the task you just saw. Two: missing $\sqrt{d_k}$ — softmax saturates, gradients vanish. Three: wrong mask type — attn_mask and key_padding_mask are different arguments with different semantics; mixing them up silently lets tokens attend to padding. Four: $O(L^2)$ memory — doubling the sequence quadruples the RAM; use FlashAttention or Longformer for very long contexts.

→ "Four paradigms, consolidated."
Slide 18~3 minFour paradigms

Consolidation: four paradigms so far

Walk the table — it's the mental filing system for the whole of M5. Flat vectors: MLP / autoencoder (5.1). Images as grids: CNN / ViT (5.2). Short sequences: LSTM / GRU (5.3). Long sequences: Transformer (today).

"When a new problem lands, ask two questions: what's the data's structure, and how long are the dependencies? This table is the answer key."

→ "Reflection."
Slide 19~2 minReflection

Reflection

Walk the checklist: derive scaled dot-product attention from Q, K, V and einsum; explain why $\sqrt{d_k}$ is essential; implement multi-head attention and sinusoidal positional encoding; assemble a TinyTransformer; compare BERT, GPT, T5, ViT at the architecture level.
