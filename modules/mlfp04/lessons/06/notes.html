Speaker Notes — Lesson 4.6 NLP — From Text to Topics

Total time: ~75 minutes  ·  20 slides  ·  ~3.5 min per slide average

Text as data. Three topic technologies — NMF, LDA, BERTopic — with honest evaluation: held-out perplexity for fit, NPMI/UMass coherence for quality, and a human read for judgment. Two discipline points to land: LDA is fitted on raw counts, never TF-IDF weights (its likelihood is over word tokens), and perplexity measures fit, not human-judged topic quality. The corpus is the real AG News one — 4,967 deduplicated stories.

Slide 1~1 minTitle

NLP — From Text to Topics

Open: "4.5 mined baskets of items. Today the baskets are documents and the items are words. Outcome: extract and evaluate topics from a real news corpus."

→ "Why text."
Slide 2~3 minWhy text

Text: the most common unstructured data

Walk the bullets: support tickets, reviews, news articles, legal contracts, chat logs. "Nobody reads them all — topic modelling discovers themes automatically, and the themes become features for downstream classification and routing."

Read the green box: "Same discovery mindset as clustering and association rules — but the 'items' are words."

→ "The pipeline, watching the data change."
Slide 3~4 minPipeline

Text analysis pipeline — watching the data change

Walk the four stages on one example sentence. Raw text: a string, no structure, one of 5,000 articles. Tokens: normalised word pieces. TF-IDF vector: 5,000 dimensions, about six nonzero — sparse bag-of-words. Topic distribution: dense, 3 dims, interpretable — Transport 0.72, Workplace 0.21, Housing 0.07.

Read the bottom annotation: "String (unstructured) → sparse vector (5,000-dim) → dense topic vector (3-dim). Every method today lives somewhere along this arrow."

→ "TF-IDF."
Slide 4~3 minTF-IDF

TF-IDF — weighting words by importance

Walk the formula: term frequency times log(N/df). tf asks how often the word appears in this document; idf asks how rare it is across all documents. "Common words — the, and — get idf ≈ 0 and contribute nothing, without a stopword list doing the deleting."

Read the green box: "Think of idf as a surprise weight. Common words are not surprising; rare words are."

→ "The smarter cousin."
Slide 5~3 minBM25

BM25 — TF-IDF's smarter cousin

Walk the two fixes. "TF-IDF is linear in term frequency — ten repeats is ten times the weight. BM25 saturates: the tenth repetition barely matters. And longer documents get penalised via length normalisation, so a 5,000-word ramble doesn't outscore a tight paragraph just by containing everything."

Land the last bullet: "This is the default ranking function in Lucene and Elasticsearch — every search box you've used today. M6's retrieval lesson will meet it again."

→ "From sparse to dense."
Slide 6~4 minEmbeddings

Word embeddings — dense semantic vectors

Walk the contrast. Sparse one-hot: "happy" and "joyful" are orthogonal — the representation knows nothing about similarity. Dense embeddings: [0.82, 0.31, −0.15] vs [0.79, 0.34, −0.12] — similar words get similar coordinates, and arithmetic on vectors becomes meaningful.

Walk the three families: Word2Vec (skip-gram / CBOW), GloVe (global co-occurrence), FastText (subword units — handles out-of-vocabulary words). Read the blue box: "'How does Word2Vec learn these vectors?' Answer in 4.8 — hidden layers are automated feature engineering."

→ "Topic modelling proper: LDA."
Slide 7~4 minLDA story

LDA — the generative story

Walk the three-step story. For each topic, draw a word distribution from Dirichlet($\beta$). For each document, draw a topic mixture from Dirichlet($\alpha$). For each word position: pick a topic from the document's mixture, then a word from that topic's distribution. "Inference reverses the story: given the words, find the most likely topics."

Read the green box: "LDA is soft clustering for documents — each document belongs to multiple topics, like GMM's soft assignments from 4.2." Then the footer — a discipline point: "The story draws whole word tokens, so LDA is fitted on raw counts, never TF-IDF weights."

→ "The plate diagram."
Slide 8~3 minPlate notation

LDA plate notation

Decode the diagram for the room: plates are loops — the outer one over $N$ documents, the inner one over each document's words. Shaded $w$ is the only observed variable; everything else is latent. Follow the arrows: $\alpha \to \theta \to z \to w \leftarrow \phi \leftarrow \beta$.

"You don't need to read plate notation fluently — you need to read this one. It says: documents are mixtures, topics are distributions, and only the words are seen. Next slide makes it concrete."

→ "Mixtures, concretely."
Slide 9~4 minMixtures picture

Documents are mixtures of topics; topics are mixtures of words

Walk the three columns. Left: five documents as stacked proportion bars — Doc 1 is mostly transport with some housing, Doc 4 is almost pure policy. Centre: the three learned topics as word distributions — mrt/delay/train, hdb/flat/price, budget/tax/gst. Right: the top words per topic, sized by probability.

Read the bottom bar: "Each document = weighted mixture of topics. Each topic = probability distribution over words. Two matrices — that's all LDA learns. The same $W$ and $H$ shapes as NMF, arriving shortly."

→ "Fit it properly."
Slide 10~4 minLDA in practice

LDA in practice: counts in, held-out perplexity out

Walk the code. Split the documents first — train and held-out. CountVectorizer produces raw counts — never TF-IDF — with stopwords removed and document-frequency bounds. Fit LDA at K = 5, 10, 20 and print held-out perplexity for each.

Read the footer: "Training-set perplexity always improves with more topics — only held-out documents tell you when $K$ starts to overfit. Same overfitting discipline as M3, in a generative model. And an implementation note: sklearn fits LDA by variational Bayes, not Gibbs sampling."

→ "The factorisation route: NMF."
Slide 11~3 minNMF

NMF — topics via matrix factorisation

Walk the decomposition: the TF-IDF matrix $X \approx WH$ with non-negative entries. $W$ rows are topic weights per document; $H$ rows are word weights per topic — the same two matrices as the mixtures picture. "Faster than LDA, sparser topics, and in practice often more interpretable."

Read the blue box: "NMF is the closest relative of PCA from 4.3 — same low-rank factorisation, different constraint: non-negativity. Non-negative parts add up; nothing cancels, which is why the topics read like word lists instead of loadings."

→ "The modern pipeline: BERTopic."
Slide 12~4 minBERTopic steps

BERTopic — what each step produces

Walk the four stages with their concrete intermediates. Step 1: BERT embeds every document into ~384 dimensions — no visible structure yet. Step 2: UMAP compresses to ~5 dims — clusters emerge (the slide draws 2-D only for plotting). Step 3: HDBSCAN labels the clusters — automatically 3 topics plus a noise pool. Step 4: c-TF-IDF names each topic — TF-IDF computed per cluster, giving the top words.

Read the bottom line: "Each step produces a concrete intermediate: embeddings → ~5-D projection → labels → topic words. Every stage is inspectable — when a topic looks wrong you can find the stage that broke it."

→ "In code."
Slide 13~4 minBERTopic code

BERTopic in practice

Walk the code. The embedder is SentenceTransformer(os.environ["TOPIC_EMBED_MODEL"]) — read from the environment, never hardcoded; it's configuration. UMAP is seeded and set to 5 components; BERTopic takes both plus min_topic_size. fit_transform returns topic labels and probabilities; get_topic_info summarises; visualize_heatmap shows topic similarity.

Read the teal box: "No Kailash wrapper here — BERTopic and its plots are used directly. The course rule is that the embedding model comes from configuration."

→ "How do you judge topics honestly?"
Slide 14~4 minCoherence

Coherence — NPMI and UMass

Walk the two metrics. NPMI in [−1, 1]: 0 means the top words co-occur at chance, above 0 means more than chance — usually measured on a reference corpus with a sliding window. UMass: document co-occurrence counts inside the training corpus, ordered pairs of top words — ≤ 0, closer to 0 is better. Average over pairs of each topic's top-10 words, then across topics.

Read the amber warning: "Perplexity measures fit, not human-judged topic quality. Choose $K$ with held-out perplexity; judge topics with coherence and a human read. A topic can be statistically optimal and semantically meaningless."

→ "The worked run on real news."
Slide 15~4 minWorked AG News

TF-IDF + NMF on AG News

Walk the code: load_corpus() gives 4,967 deduplicated AG News stories; TF-IDF at 5,000 features with min_df 5 and max_df 0.7; NMF at 10 components with the nndsvd initialisation. Print the top six words per topic.

Read the actual output: "oil, prices, stocks, crude" — business; "athens, gold, olympic, phelps" — sport; "najaf, iraq, sadr, cleric" — world; "microsoft, windows, xp, update" — tech. Four of AG News's four families, named by the machine. Note the Kailash equivalent on the slide: DimReductionEngine().reduce(..., algorithm="nmf") on a dense polars frame — same factorisation, course API.

→ "The supervised cousin: sentiment."
Slide 16~4 minSentiment

Sentiment analysis — text classification

Walk the three bullets. Supervised: text → TF-IDF or embedding features → an M3 classifier; targets like positive/negative or 1–5 stars. The unsupervised connection: word vectors learned from unlabelled text — co-occurrence → PPMI → SVD, the factorisation Word2Vec implicitly performs — averaged into document features. And the label discipline: "Labels must come from people — a lexicon applied to the same text just teaches the classifier the lexicon."

Read the green box: "Exercise 6.5: learn PPMI + SVD word vectors, train on human-labelled SST-2 movie-review sentences, test on unseen sentences against a lexicon and a TF-IDF baseline — all on the same split."

→ "Three methods, one table."
Slide 17~3 minMethods compared

Three methods compared

Walk the table. TF-IDF + NMF: sparse representation, you set K, fast and interpretable — the default. LDA: sparse, you set K, soft assignments and a generative story — when you need document mixtures as probabilities. BERTopic: dense BERT embeddings, automatic topic count, semantic and modern — when the corpus is big enough and semantics matter.

Read the closing line: "Compare all three on the same corpus. Evaluate with NPMI. Pick the one that best matches your downstream need — not the one with the fanciest name."

→ "Four mistakes."
Slide 18~3 minMistakes

Four common mistakes

Walk the four. One: skipping stopword removal — your top words become "the", "and", "to". Useless. Two: wrong $K$ — start 10–20, tune with coherence. Three: judging topics by perplexity alone — it measures fit, not human-judged quality, and training-set perplexity always prefers more topics. Four: BERTopic on tiny corpora — it needs hundreds of documents; fall back to NMF.

→ "Connections."
Slide 19~2 minConnections

Connections

Walk the four links. 4.3: NMF is non-negative PCA. 4.5: topic modelling is association mining for words. 4.7: NMF factorises doc-word; matrix factorisation factorises user-item — same math. M5: BERTopic's sentence embeddings come from frozen BERT — M5 trains those.
